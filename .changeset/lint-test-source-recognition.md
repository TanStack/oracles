'@tanstack/eslint-plugin-oracles': patch
---

`oracle/no-unit-test`, `oracle/no-disabled`, and `oracle/oracle-property-only` now decide whether a callee is a test function by where its binding comes from, not by its name alone. Before, any package's export named `it`, `test`, `describe`, `suite`, or `bench` was a test function, so Ramda's `test` and mitata's `bench` were reported. A default import was never a test function, so `import test from 'ava'`, `'node:test'`, or `'@playwright/test'` was silent, and so were `xit`, `fit`, node:test's `skip` and `only`, `Deno.test`, `require('node:test')`, and fixtures with other names.

- The rules know the test frameworks: Vitest (and `@vitest/runner`, `vite-plus/test`, `@fast-check/vitest`, `@effect/vitest`), Playwright and its component-testing packages, Jest (`@jest/globals`, `jest-each`, `@fast-check/jest`), Mocha and `mocha-steps`, node:test (and the `test` package), AVA (and `@fast-check/ava`), tap, tape, zora, brittle, uvu, `bun:test`, `@std/testing/bdd`, QUnit, Japa, and Lab. Each framework's default, named, namespace, and `require` exports count, with the modifiers that an export carries (`xit`, `fit`, `skip`, `only`, `todo`) and its factories (`mergeTests`, uvu's `suite`, `jest-each`, Lab's `script`). Members such as `.fail`, `.failing`, `.expectFailure`, `.ignore`, `.if`, `.todoIf`, `.override`, `.scoped`, and `.effect` keep a registration. The globals of the Vitest, Jest, Mocha, and Jasmine environments count too, and so do `Deno.test` and `QUnit.test`.
- The rules know packages that export other functions under these names, such as Ramda, Rambda, remeda, lodash, mitata, benny, `@thi.ng/*`, and `@tensorflow/*`. Their exports are never reported.
- The rules read a project's own modules: relative modules, `#` imports through the nearest `package.json` `imports` (a target that names a package is classified as that package), and workspace packages (a `node_modules` link to a real path outside `node_modules`), through their `exports`. They never read a package under `node_modules`; a project whose own path is under a `node_modules` directory is still read.
- `settings.oracles.testPackages` maps a specifier to a framework's specifier or to a table of export roles, and `settings.oracles.nonTestPackages` lists packages without test functions. Both are read first. A setting that the rules cannot use, including a `settings.oracles` that is not an object, is reported as `malformedSourceSetting` and read as unset; it never makes a rule throw.
- Any other source is one the rules cannot classify, whatever the import's name. By default (`settings.oracles.unknownSources: 'report'`), `oracle/no-unit-test` reports `unknownTestSource` once per import, at the import, for a title-shaped call through it (a string title, then a function), and for a call to a relative helper that makes one. A call with no title before its function, such as `pMap(xs, fn)` or `effect(fn)`, gets none. Machinery files (`oracle-*`) get the report too. `'test'` reads a framework's name from such a source as the framework's, and `'ignore'` reads nothing as a test function.
- A registration's body may come first: every rule finds the function in `test(fn)` and `test.skip(condition, description)`.
- The README's new "Decisions" section records the maintainer decision of 2026-11-12 and the limits it accepts.
